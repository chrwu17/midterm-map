The U.S. House of Representatives is one half of Congress. The other half is the Senate. A bill has to pass both before the president can sign it.

## The basics

- The House has 435 voting members. The number is fixed by law at no more than 435, and the seats are divided among the 50 states by population.
- Terms last two years. The Constitution says members are "chosen every second Year by the People," so every House seat is on the ballot in every federal election year, including midterms.
- Each member speaks for one district. A state with several seats is split into districts, and the voters in each district pick one representative.
- Six states have only one seat: Alaska, Delaware, North Dakota, South Dakota, Vermont and Wyoming. The whole state votes for that one representative, who is called "at large."
- A representative must be at least 25 years old, have been a U.S. citizen for seven years, and live in the state they represent when elected.

The Constitution calls for a head count of the whole country, the census, every ten years. How many seats each state gets depends on its population. You can read the rules in [Article I of the Constitution](https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/constitution-transcript) and see every member in the [House directory](https://www.house.gov/representatives).

## What representatives do

According to the House's own website, representatives introduce bills and resolutions, offer amendments, serve on committees and vote. They also attend meetings and hearings and read mail from people in their district.

Each committee covers a topic, looks at bills and recommends which ones the full House should take up. Committees also oversee the agencies and programs in their area.

The House picks its own leader, the Speaker, who runs the House and holds several leadership and administrative roles.

## Powers that belong to the House

The Constitution gives the House two powers that the Senate does not share:

- Bills for raising revenue, which means taxes, must start in the House.
- The House has "the sole Power of Impeachment." It can impeach an official, and then the Senate holds the trial.

## Delegates

Five delegates also serve in the House for the District of Columbia, the Virgin Islands, Guam, American Samoa and the Northern Mariana Islands. A resident commissioner represents Puerto Rico. They are not counted among the 435 voting members.

## The 2026 election

All 435 House seats were on the ballot on November 3, 2026. Each state page on this site lists the state's House districts with the candidates, their parties, the vote shares and the winner. Unlike the Senate and governor races, we did not research House candidates' positions, so those pages show results only. Results stay unofficial until each state certifies them.

## How to find your representative

Go to [house.gov](https://www.house.gov/representatives/find-your-representative) and enter your ZIP code. You will see who represents your district and how to contact them.

## Key takeaways

- The House has 435 voting members, and each one speaks for a single district, or for a whole state if the state has only one seat.
- Representatives serve two-year terms, so all 435 seats are up in every federal election year.
- Representatives write and vote on bills, serve on committees and hear from people in their district.
- Tax bills start in the House, and only the House can impeach an official.
- You can look up your representative on house.gov with your ZIP code.
