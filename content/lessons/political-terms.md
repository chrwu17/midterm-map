Political talk is full of jargon. Here are common terms, in simple language. For more, the [Senate glossary](https://www.senate.gov/reference/glossary_term/glossary.htm) and [Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/) are helpful.

- **Incumbent:** The person who currently holds an office and is running to keep it.
- **Challenger:** A candidate trying to unseat the incumbent.
- **Open seat:** An office with no incumbent running, often because the officeholder retired or hit a term limit.
- **Primary:** An election where voters pick a party's nominee for the general election. Rules on who can vote vary by state.
- **General election:** The main election where nominees compete for the office. In 2026 it was on November 3.
- **Runoff:** A second election between the top vote-getters when no one reaches the required share in the first round. Only some states use them.
- **Special election:** An election held outside the regular schedule to fill a seat that became empty early.
- **Ranked-choice voting:** A system where you rank candidates in order of preference, and if no one wins a majority, the last-place candidate is dropped and their voters' next choices are counted until someone does.
- **Called:** A race is called when major news outlets project a winner from the votes counted so far, which happens before the official, certified result.
- **Midterm:** An election held halfway through a president's term.
- **Ballot measure:** A question put straight to voters, such as a proposed law or constitutional change. Also called a referendum or initiative.
- **Precinct:** A small local area whose voters usually go to the same polling place.
- **Polling place:** The location where you vote in person.
- **Absentee ballot:** A ballot you fill out and return by mail instead of voting in person.
- **Provisional ballot:** A backup ballot given when there's a question about your eligibility. It counts once officials confirm you can vote.
- **Caucus:** A meeting where party members gather and show support for candidates. It can also mean a group of lawmakers with shared interests, like a state delegation.
- **Majority:** More than half. In Congress, the party with the most seats controls the "majority."
- **Filibuster:** A Senate tactic to delay or block a vote by extended debate. Ending debate on most bills takes 60 votes, per [Senate rules](https://www.senate.gov/artandhistory/history/common/briefing/Cloture_Rule.htm).
- **Veto:** When a president or governor rejects a bill passed by the legislature. Lawmakers may override it with a larger vote.
- **Gerrymandering:** Drawing district lines to favor a party or group. Both major parties have been accused of it. [NCSL](https://www.ncsl.org/redistricting-and-census) explains redistricting.
- **Constituent:** A person an elected official represents.
- **Electoral calendar:** The schedule of key election dates, such as registration deadlines, primaries, early voting, and Election Day. Check yours at [vote.gov](https://vote.gov).
- **Nonpartisan:** Not favoring any political party.

## Tips for learning more

If you hear a word you do not know, look it up before you decide what you think. Candidates sometimes use terms to sound impressive or to stay vague. A plain definition lets you see what they actually said.

## Key takeaways

- An incumbent holds the office now. An open seat has no incumbent running.
- Primaries choose party nominees. General elections choose winners.
- Runoffs and special elections happen outside the usual pattern.
- Veto and filibuster are tools to block or delay action.
- An electoral calendar lists your key deadlines, so check it often.
