A midterm election happens in the middle of a president's four-year term. The president is not on the ballot, but Congress and many state offices are.

## What is on the ballot

In every midterm, all 435 seats in the U.S. House of Representatives are up for election. House members serve two-year terms, so they are always running soon. You can read an overview in the [USA.gov guide to congressional and midterm elections](https://www.usa.gov/midterm-elections).

About one-third of the 100 U.S. Senate seats are also up, because senators serve six-year terms that are staggered. (Our [Senate lesson](/learn/what-a-senator-does) explains how.)

Most states also elect governors in midterm years. Many states vote on state legislators, attorneys general, judges, mayors, and local ballot questions at the same time.

## Why midterms matter

Congress writes federal laws, sets the federal budget, and checks the president. Who controls the House and Senate shapes which bills move forward and which stall. Governors and state legislators decide a lot of everyday rules too, like school funding, road projects, and many election procedures.

Midterms also tend to have lower turnout than presidential elections. That means each vote counts for a bigger share of the total. The [U.S. Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/topics/public-sector/voting.html) publishes turnout data if you want to see the numbers.

## The 2026 example

The 2026 midterms were held on Tuesday, November 3, 2026. The [Constitution](https://constitution.congress.gov/constitution/article-1/) sets two-year House terms, and federal law puts Election Day on the Tuesday after the first Monday in November.

In 2026, voters in these places made choices:

- All 435 House seats
- 35 Senate seats (33 regular seats plus two special elections, for Ohio and Florida)
- 36 governorships (see [Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Gubernatorial_elections,_2026))
- Thousands of state and local offices

Before the general election, most states held primaries, where voters picked each party's nominee. Dates differ by state.

## Why "midterm" and not "off-year"?

People sometimes call these "off-year" elections, but that is a different thing. "Midterm" just means the election falls in the middle of a presidential term. Congress is elected every two years no matter what.

## Key takeaways

- A midterm is held halfway through a president's term, and the president is not on the ballot.
- All 435 House seats and about one-third of Senate seats are up every midterm.
- Most states also elect governors and many local officials in midterm years.
- Turnout is usually lower than in presidential years, so each vote counts for more.
- The 2026 midterms took place on November 3, 2026.
